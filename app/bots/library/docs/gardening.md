# Growing food

## When to plant (hardiness zones and frost dates)

Hardiness zones rate how cold winters get: Canada's run from 0 (coldest) to
9, the USDA's from 1 to 13, and the two are close but not identical. For
vegetables the dates that matter are your average last spring frost and first
fall frost; the days between them are your growing season. Plant
frost-tender crops (tomatoes, peppers, squash, beans, cucumbers, corn) after
the last frost; tomatoes and peppers do best once nights stay above about
10 °C; hardy ones (peas, lettuce, spinach, kale, radish, onions,
potatoes) can be planted 2 to 4 weeks before it.

## Easy vegetables for beginners

Radishes (ready in 25 to 30 days), lettuce and salad greens (30 to 50 days,
pick leaves as needed), bush beans (50 to 60 days), peas (60 days, early
spring), zucchini (very productive), kale and Swiss chard (pick all season),
potatoes, and garlic (planted in fall, harvested next summer). Herbs such as
parsley, chives, mint (keep it in a pot, it spreads) and basil are easy too.
Tomatoes are easiest bought as young plants.

## Starting seeds indoors

Start tomatoes and peppers 6 to 8 weeks before the last frost, squash and
cucumbers 3 to 4 weeks before. Use a light seed-starting mix, sow about 2 to
3 times as deep as the seed is wide, keep it moist and warm (20 to 25 °C) until
they sprout, then give them strong light (a sunny window or a grow light
about 5 to 10 cm above them for 14 to 16 hours a day). Harden them off before
planting out: put them outside a few hours a day for a week, increasing
little by little.

## Planting depth, spacing and watering

Sow seeds about 2 to 3 times as deep as their width; tiny seeds (lettuce,
carrots) barely covered. Follow the spacing on the packet and thin seedlings
so each has room. Most vegetables need about 2.5 cm (1 inch) of water a week:
water deeply at the base in the morning, less often rather than a little
every day. Mulch (straw, leaves, grass clippings) keeps moisture in and
weeds down. Most vegetables want 6 to 8 hours of sun; leafy greens cope with
less.

## Growing potatoes

Plant seed potatoes (or sprouted store potatoes, though disease is more
likely), cut into pieces with at least 2 eyes each and dried for a day, about
10 cm deep and 30 cm apart, 2 to 4 weeks before the last frost. When the
plants are 20 cm tall, pile soil or mulch around the stems (hilling) to keep
the potatoes covered: sunlight turns them green and toxic. New potatoes come
after flowering; main crop when the plants die back. Cure in a dark place for
1 to 2 weeks before storing.

## Composting

Mix "browns" (dry leaves, straw, cardboard, paper) with "greens" (vegetable
scraps, coffee grounds, grass clippings) at about 2 to 3 parts browns to 1 part
greens by volume. Keep it as moist as a wrung-out sponge and turn it every
week or two for air; a hot pile is ready in 2 to 3 months, a cold one in 6 to
12. Leave out meat, fish, dairy, oil, pet waste and diseased plants. A bad
smell means too wet or too many greens: add browns and turn it.

## Saving seeds

Save from open-pollinated or heirloom varieties: seeds from hybrid (F1)
plants do not grow true. Easiest: beans, peas, tomatoes and lettuce, which
mostly pollinate themselves. Let bean and pea pods dry on the plant. Tomato
seeds: scoop them out, let them ferment in a little water for 2 to 3 days,
rinse and dry. Dry all seeds well, then store them in labelled envelopes in a
cool, dry, dark place; most keep 2 to 5 years.

## Garden pests without chemicals

Pick off larger pests (beetles, caterpillars, slugs) by hand, early morning
or evening. Spray aphids off with water or with a mild soap solution (a
teaspoon of plain liquid soap per litre). Floating row covers keep insects
off. Beer traps catch slugs. Rotate crops each year, and grow flowers that
attract helpful insects (marigolds, dill, alyssum).

## Growing food in containers and small spaces

Use the largest pots you can, with drainage holes, and potting mix rather than
garden soil. Good in containers: lettuce, herbs, cherry tomatoes, peppers,
bush beans, radishes, and potatoes in a large bag or bucket. Containers dry
out fast: water daily in hot weather. Sprouts (lentils, mung beans, alfalfa)
grow on a windowsill in a jar in 3 to 5 days with only rinsing twice a day.

## Protecting plants from frost

When frost is forecast (clear, calm nights below about 5 °C at sunset), cover
tender plants in the evening with sheets, row cover or buckets, reaching the
ground to trap its heat; remove them in the morning. Water the soil during the
day: moist soil holds more heat. Plastic touching leaves does not protect
them.
