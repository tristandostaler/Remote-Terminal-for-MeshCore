# Cooking staples (no recipe book)

## How to cook rice (white rice)

Rinse the rice until the water runs nearly clear. Use about 1 cup of long
grain rice to 1.5 cups of water (a little less for basmati and jasmine, up to
2 cups for short grain), and a pinch of salt. Bring to a boil, cover with a tight lid, turn
to the lowest heat and simmer for 15 to 18 minutes without lifting the lid.
Turn off the heat and let it rest covered for 10 minutes, then fluff.

## How to cook brown rice

Use about 1 cup of brown rice to 2 to 2.5 cups of water. Bring to a boil,
cover, and simmer on low for 40 to 45 minutes, then rest covered for 10
minutes. Or cook it like pasta in plenty of boiling water for about 30
minutes and drain.

## How to cook dried beans

Sort out stones and rinse. Soak in plenty of cold water for 8 hours or
overnight, or quick-soak: boil 2 minutes, then leave covered for an hour.
Drain, cover with fresh water by a few centimetres, and simmer until tender:
about 1 to 2 hours depending on the bean (chickpeas can take longer). Salt
toward the end. Red kidney beans must boil hard for at least 10 minutes
first: undercooked or slow-cooker-only kidney beans are toxic.

## How to cook lentils and split peas

No soaking needed. Rinse, then simmer in about 3 cups of water per cup:
red lentils 15 to 20 minutes (they fall apart, good for soup), green or brown
lentils 20 to 30 minutes, split peas 30 to 45 minutes.

## How to cook oats (porridge)

Rolled oats: 1 part oats to 2 parts water or milk and a pinch of salt;
simmer about 5 minutes, stirring. Steel-cut oats: 1 to 3 or 4, simmer 20 to
30 minutes, or soak overnight to cut the cooking time. Overnight oats need no
cooking: soak rolled oats in milk or water in a cool place overnight.

## How to cook pasta

Use plenty of water (about 1 litre per 100 g of pasta) with a good pinch of
salt. Add pasta when the water is at a full boil, stir in the first minute,
and boil uncovered for the time on the packet, usually 8 to 12 minutes,
until just tender. Save a cup of the cooking water to loosen the sauce.

## Bread without yeast (soda bread)

Mix 500 g (about 4 cups) of flour, 1 teaspoon of baking soda and 1 teaspoon
of salt. Add about 400 ml of buttermilk, or milk with 1 tablespoon of lemon
juice or vinegar stirred in. Mix quickly into a soft dough without
kneading, shape a round, cut a deep cross on top, and bake at 200 °C
(400 °F) for 30 to 40 minutes, until it sounds hollow when tapped underneath.

## How to make flatbread in a pan (no oven)

Mix 2 cups of flour, a pinch of salt, 1 teaspoon of baking powder if you
have it, a spoon of oil, and about 3/4 cup of water into a soft dough. Rest
it 15 minutes, divide into balls, roll or press thin, and cook each in a hot
dry pan for 1 to 2 minutes per side until puffed with brown spots. Bannock is
the same dough thicker, cooked slowly in a pan or on a stick over a fire.

## Cooking with less fuel

Always use a lid: it saves up to a third of the fuel. Soak beans, rice and
grains first to cut the cooking time. Retained-heat (haybox) cooking: bring
the pot to a boil for a few minutes, then wrap it tightly in blankets or
sleeping bags, or put it in an insulated box, and leave it for a few hours;
it finishes cooking in its own heat. Keep it hot enough: eat it right away or
reheat it to a boil.

## Safe cooking temperatures

Cook to these internal temperatures: poultry 74 °C (165 °F), ground meat 71
°C (160 °F), pork, beef and lamb steaks and roasts 63 °C (145 °F) then rest 3
minutes, fish 63 °C (145 °F) or until it flakes, leftovers reheated to 74 °C
(165 °F). Without a thermometer: poultry juices run clear and no pink
remains. Keep cooked food out of the 4 to 60 °C (40 to 140 °F) danger zone
for no more than 2 hours.

## Cooking measures and substitutions

1 cup = about 240 ml; 1 tablespoon = 15 ml = 3 teaspoons; 1 cup of flour
weighs about 125 g, of sugar about 200 g, of rice about 185 g. Egg
substitute for baking: 1 tablespoon of ground flax or chia seed plus 3
tablespoons of water, rested 5 minutes. Buttermilk substitute: 1 cup of milk
with 1 tablespoon of lemon juice or vinegar, rested 5 minutes. Self-raising
flour: 1 cup flour plus 1.5 teaspoons of baking powder and a pinch of salt.
