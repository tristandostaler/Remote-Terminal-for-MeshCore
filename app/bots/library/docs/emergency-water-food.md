# Water, food and sanitation (emergency notes)

## How much water to store

Plan on 4 litres (about 1 gallon) per person per day for drinking and basic
hygiene: at least 3 days' worth, ideally two weeks. More for heat, illness,
pregnancy and nursing. Pets need water too. Store in clean food-grade
containers, away from sunlight and chemicals, and replace every 6 months.

## Where to find water at home

Safe to drink: water already stored, the hot water heater tank (turn off its
power or gas first, let it cool, drain from the valve at the bottom), water in
the pipes (open the highest tap in the house, then drain from the lowest),
melted ice cubes, and liquid in canned fruit and vegetables. The toilet tank
(not the bowl) is usable only after purifying and only if no cleaning
chemicals were added. Not for drinking: toilet bowls, waterbeds, pools and
hot tubs (use those for washing and flushing).

## Purify water by boiling

Boiling is the most reliable method. If the water is cloudy, let it settle
and filter it through a clean cloth or coffee filter first. Bring it to a
rolling boil for 1 minute; above 2,000 m (6,500 feet) elevation, boil for 3
minutes. Let it cool, and store it in clean covered containers. Pouring it
between two containers improves the flat taste.

## Purify water with bleach

Use plain unscented household bleach (sodium hypochlorite), not scented,
colour-safe or with added cleaners. For bleach of 6 percent: 2 drops per
litre, or 8 drops per gallon (4 litres). For 8.25 percent: 2 drops per litre,
or 6 drops per gallon. Double the dose if the water is cloudy, coloured or
very cold. Stir and wait 30 minutes. It should smell slightly of chlorine; if
not, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes. Bleach does not remove
chemicals or fuel.

## Purify water with tablets, filters and sunlight

Water purification tablets (chlorine dioxide or iodine): follow the package
dose and wait time; iodine is not for pregnant people or long-term use.
Portable filters remove bacteria and parasites but not always viruses:
combine with boiling or chlorine when unsure. Solar disinfection (SODIS) for
clear water only: fill a clear plastic PET bottle of 2 litres or less, lay it
in full sun for 6 hours, or 2 days if the sky is mostly cloudy.

## Water that cannot be made safe

Water contaminated with fuel, toxic chemicals or floodwater with sewage
cannot be made safe by boiling or bleach. Boiling concentrates chemicals. Find
another source.

## Fridge and freezer food during a power outage

Keep the doors closed. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours. A
full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, a half-full one for
about 24. Throw out perishable food (meat, fish, dairy, eggs, leftovers,
cooked food) that has been above 4 °C (40 °F) for more than 2 hours. Frozen
food that still has ice crystals or is at 4 °C or colder can be refrozen.
When in doubt, throw it out: never taste food to check it.

## Emergency food supply

Store food that needs no refrigeration and little water or cooking: canned
meat, fish, beans, fruit and vegetables, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit,
nuts, granola bars, cereal, powdered milk, and a manual can opener. Plan for
at least 3 days, ideally two weeks, and about 2,000 calories per adult per
day. Remember infant formula, special diets and pet food. Rotate stock by
date.

## Canned food safety

Throw away cans that are bulging, leaking, deeply dented (especially on the
seams), badly rusted, or that spurt liquid or smell bad when opened. Cans
that were in floodwater can be kept only if undamaged: remove labels, wash,
disinfect in a bleach solution, and relabel.

## Cooking without power

Cook only outdoors with barbecues, camp stoves, charcoal or generators: they
produce carbon monoxide, which kills indoors, even in a garage with the door
open. Indoors, only appliances rated for indoor use. Keep a fire extinguisher
nearby and never leave a flame unattended.

## Emergency toilet and sanitation

If toilets cannot flush: line a bucket or the toilet bowl with two heavy
plastic bags, add a little sawdust, cat litter or garden lime after each use
to control smell, and tie off and store bags away from people until they can
be removed. A toilet can still be flushed by pouring a bucket of water into
the bowl if sewers work. Bury waste outdoors in a hole 15 to 20 cm deep, at
least 60 m (200 feet) from water sources.

## Handwashing and hygiene

Wash hands with soap and clean water for 20 seconds before handling food or
water, after the toilet, and after caring for the sick. Without water, use
hand sanitizer of at least 60 percent alcohol. A tippy tap (a hanging jug
tipped by a foot lever) saves water. Keep drinking water containers covered
and use a clean ladle rather than dipping hands.
