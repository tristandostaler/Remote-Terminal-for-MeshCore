# Practical repairs (car and home)

## Jump-starting a car

Turn both cars off. Connect in this order: red clamp to the dead battery's
positive (+), other red clamp to the good battery's positive (+), black clamp
to the good battery's negative (-), and the last black clamp to bare,
unpainted metal on the dead car's engine block, away from the battery. Start
the good car, wait a few minutes, then start the dead one. Remove the clamps
in reverse order, without letting them touch. Drive at least 20 to 30
minutes to recharge. Do not jump a cracked, leaking or frozen battery.

## Changing a flat tire

Park on flat, firm ground away from traffic, hazard lights on, parking brake
on, and block the wheel diagonally opposite. Loosen the wheel nuts about half
a turn while the wheel is still on the ground. Jack the car at the jacking
point shown in the manual, never get under it. Remove the nuts and wheel, fit
the spare, hand-tighten the nuts, lower the car, then tighten firmly in a
star (criss-cross) pattern. Check the spare's pressure. A small "donut" spare
is limited to about 80 km/h (50 mph) and a short distance; recheck nut
tightness after 50 km.

## Car will not start

Clicking or nothing when turning the key: usually a flat battery; check the
terminals are clean and tight, then jump-start. Engine cranks but will not
start: check fuel, and on older cars wait a few minutes if you smell fuel
(flooded). Battery warning light while driving: the alternator is not
charging; you have limited time before the car stops. Overheating: stop, turn
the engine off, and never open a hot radiator cap.

## Frozen pipes

Signs: little or no water from a tap in cold weather. Open the tap so water
can flow as it melts. Warm the pipe starting near the tap and working back,
with a hair dryer, a heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water: never an
open flame. If you cannot find or reach the frozen part, or the pipe has
cracked, shut off the main water valve and call a plumber. Prevention: let
taps drip, open cabinet doors under sinks, and insulate pipes.

## Burst or leaking pipe

Shut off the main water valve right away (often where the pipe enters the
house, or at the meter; clockwise to close). Turn off electricity in the
area if water is near outlets or appliances, but not while standing in
water. Open taps to drain the pipes. A temporary fix for a small leak: a pipe
repair clamp, rubber sheet held with hose clamps, or epoxy putty, until it
can be repaired properly.

## Tripped breaker or blown fuse

Unplug or switch off the appliances on the dead circuit first. At the panel,
a tripped breaker sits in the middle or off position: push it fully off, then
back on. If it trips again immediately, there is a fault: leave it off and
call an electrician. Replace a blown fuse only with one of the same rating,
never a bigger one, and never bypass it. A GFCI or RCD outlet that cut out
resets with its reset button.

## Clogged drain or toilet

Toilet: use a flange plunger with a good seal and firm push-pull strokes;
stop flushing if the bowl is near full. Sink: remove and clean the stopper,
use a cup plunger (block the overflow hole with a wet cloth), or clean the
U-shaped trap under the sink with a bucket below it. Hot (not boiling) water
and dish soap help with grease. Avoid mixing chemical drain cleaners: some
combinations release toxic gas.

## Broken window or roof leak (temporary fix)

Broken window: wear gloves, remove loose glass, then cover the opening with
plywood, cardboard or heavy plastic sheeting taped and stapled to the frame.
Roof leak: put a bucket under the drip and poke a small hole in a bulging
ceiling to drain water in a controlled way. From outside, only in safe dry
weather, cover the damaged area with a tarp extending past the ridge,
weighted or nailed with wooden battens.

## Water heater and appliances after a power cut

After power returns, turn appliances back on one at a time to avoid a surge.
A gas water heater or furnace pilot light may need relighting following the
instructions on the unit; if you smell gas, do not light anything and leave.
An electric water heater that was drained must be refilled before its power
is turned back on, or the heating element burns out.
