# Power, home safety and severe weather (emergency notes)

## Generator safety

Run a generator only outdoors, at least 6 m (20 feet) from doors, windows
and vents, with the exhaust pointing away from buildings: never in a house,
garage, basement or shed, even with the doors open. Never plug a generator
into a wall outlet (backfeeding): it can kill utility workers and neighbours.
Use heavy-duty outdoor extension cords, or a transfer switch installed by an
electrician. Let it cool before refuelling, and store fuel outside the house.

## Carbon monoxide poisoning

Carbon monoxide (CO) has no smell or colour. It comes from generators,
barbecues, camp stoves, cars and fuel heaters used indoors or in garages.
Signs: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion, and several people
or pets feeling ill at once. Get everyone outside into fresh air immediately,
then call for help. Use a battery-powered CO alarm.

## Power outage

When the power goes out, unplug electronics and appliances to protect them from the surge when power
returns; leave one lamp on to know when it is back. Use flashlights rather
than candles. Keep fridge and freezer closed. Stay away from downed power
lines, at least 10 m (33 feet): assume they are live, and never drive over
them. If a line falls on your car, stay inside unless it is on fire.

## Charging phones and radios without power

Save battery: low screen brightness, airplane or battery-saver mode, close
apps, turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and location when unused. Charge from a car
only while the car runs outdoors (never in a closed garage), power banks,
small solar panels, or hand-crank radios. A MeshCore node lasts longer with a
lower advert frequency and a solar panel.

## Gas leak

Signs: smell of rotten eggs, hissing sound, dead plants near a gas line. Leave
the building immediately, leaving doors open. Do not switch lights or
appliances on or off, light a flame, or use a phone inside. From outside,
call the gas company or emergency services. Turn gas off at the meter only if
you know how and it is safe; once it is off, only the utility should turn it
back on.

## Shut off water and electricity

Know where your main water valve is (often near where the pipe enters the
house, or at the meter): turn it clockwise to close, useful when pipes burst
or the supply is contaminated. Turn off electricity at the main breaker or
fuse box if there is flooding, sparks or a burning smell, but never while
standing in water.

## Heating during a winter outage

Choose one small room, close its doors, cover windows with blankets and
cracks with towels. Wear layers, a hat, and sleep in sleeping bags together.
Use only heaters rated for indoor use, with ventilation and a CO alarm;
never use an oven, barbecue or camp stove to heat a room. Let taps drip to
keep pipes from freezing.

## Fire in the home

Get out, stay out, and call for help from outside. Crawl low under smoke.
Feel doors with the back of your hand before opening; if hot, use another
way out. If clothes catch fire: stop, drop and roll. Small pan fire: turn off
the heat and cover with a lid; never use water on oil or grease. Fire
extinguisher use (PASS): Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze
the handle, Sweep side to side.

## Earthquake

During shaking: drop to your hands and knees, cover your head and neck under a
sturdy table or next to an interior wall, hold on until it stops. Stay
indoors; do not run outside or stand in a doorway. Outdoors: move away from
buildings, trees and power lines. In a car: stop away from bridges and
overpasses and stay in. Afterwards expect aftershocks, check for gas leaks and
damage, and near the coast move to high ground in case of a tsunami.

## Flood and flash flood

Move to higher ground. Turn around, don't drown: never walk or drive through
flood water. How deep is dangerous: 15 cm (6 inches) of moving water can knock you down, 30 cm
(1 foot) can float a car, and 60 cm (2 feet) can sweep away most vehicles.
Floodwater may hide holes, debris and live wires, and carries sewage. If
trapped in a building, go to the highest level, but not a closed attic;
signal from the roof if needed.

## Tornado

During a tornado warning, go to a basement or storm shelter, or an interior room without windows on the
lowest floor (a bathroom or closet), and cover your head and neck. Stay away
from windows. Mobile homes and cars are not safe: leave for a sturdy
building. If caught outside, lie flat in a ditch or low area and protect your
head.

## Thunderstorm and lightning

When thunder roars, go indoors: if you hear thunder, you are close enough to
be struck. Shelter in a building or a hard-topped car, not under trees, in
open fields, or on hilltops. Stay indoors until 30 minutes after the last
thunder. Distance to lightning: count the seconds from flash to thunder and
divide by 3 for kilometres, or by 5 for miles. Avoid corded phones, plumbing
and water during the storm.

## Hurricane and high wind

Bring in or tie down outdoor objects. Taping windows does not help: close
storm shutters or board the windows, then stay in an interior room away from
windows. Follow
evacuation orders. Do not go out during the calm eye of the storm: the wind
returns from the other direction.

## Wildfire and smoke

Be ready to evacuate early, with a go bag and the car fuelled and facing out.
Close windows, doors and vents; move flammable items away from the house. If
trapped, stay in a building or car with the engine running and vents closed,
away from windows, and lie low. For smoke, stay indoors with windows closed,
use a HEPA air purifier if available, and wear an N95 mask outside.

## Extreme heat

Stay indoors in the coolest room, drink water regularly even if not thirsty,
avoid alcohol and heavy activity in the hottest hours, and take cool showers.
Check on elderly neighbours, young children and people with chronic illness.
Never leave children or pets in a parked car.

## Go bag checklist

Water (4 litres per person per day), non-perishable food and a can opener,
flashlight and spare batteries, battery or hand-crank radio, first-aid kit,
medications and copies of prescriptions, whistle, dust masks, plastic
sheeting and tape, multi-tool, phone chargers and power bank, cash in small
bills, copies of IDs and important documents, warm clothing and a blanket,
sturdy shoes, hygiene items, local paper map, and supplies for babies, pets
and anyone with special needs.
