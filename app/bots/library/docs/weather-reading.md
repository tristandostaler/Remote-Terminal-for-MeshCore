# Reading the weather without a forecast

## Barometer and air pressure trends

The trend over 3 hours matters more than the number. Falling fast (more than
3 hPa in 3 hours): wind and rain or a storm likely within hours. Falling
slowly: weather worsening over the next day. Steady: little change. Rising:
clearing and improving; rising fast can bring strong gusty wind. High
pressure (above about 1020 hPa) usually means settled weather, low (below
about 1000 hPa) unsettled. Most phones have a barometer an app can read.

## Cloud types and what they mean

Cirrus (high, thin, wispy "mares' tails"): fair now, but if they thicken
into a milky sheet (cirrostratus, often with a halo around the sun or moon)
rain or snow may arrive within 12 to 24 hours. Altocumulus (mid-level puffy
rows, "mackerel sky"): change on the way. Cumulus (fluffy, flat-bottomed, fair
weather) are fine unless they grow tall by afternoon. Cumulonimbus (towering,
anvil-shaped top): thunderstorm, heavy rain, hail, lightning and gusts.
Nimbostratus (thick grey low layer): steady rain or snow. Stratus (low grey
sheet): drizzle or fog.

## Signs that a storm or thunderstorm is coming

Cumulus clouds growing tall and dark in the afternoon, an anvil top, a sudden
cool gusty wind, the sky darkening, thunder or lightning on the horizon,
static on AM radio. Count seconds from flash to thunder: divide by 3 for
kilometres, by 5 for miles. Under 30 seconds (10 km), go indoors.

## Wind direction and weather changes

In the northern hemisphere, with your back to the wind, low pressure is to
your left (Buys Ballot's law). A wind backing (turning counter-clockwise, for
example from west to south) often means a low and bad weather approaching; a
wind veering (turning clockwise, for example from south to west or northwest)
often follows a cold front and brings clearing and cooler air. Calm, clear
nights bring frost and fog.

## Beaufort wind scale

0 calm, smoke rises straight (under 1 km/h). 2 light breeze, leaves rustle
(6 to 11 km/h). 4 moderate, dust and small branches move (20 to 28 km/h). 6
strong breeze, large branches move, umbrellas hard to use (39 to 49 km/h). 7
near gale, whole trees move, hard to walk against (50 to 61 km/h). 8 gale,
twigs break off (62 to 74 km/h). 10 storm, trees uprooted (89 to 102 km/h).
12 hurricane force (118 km/h and above).

## Weather sayings that work

"Red sky at night, shepherd's delight; red sky in the morning, shepherd's
warning": in mid latitudes where weather comes from the west, it often holds.
A halo around the sun or moon often means rain or snow within a day. Dew or
frost on the grass in the morning means a clear night and usually a fair day;
none on a clear morning can mean clouds or wind arriving. Smoke that hangs
low and drifts along the ground suggests falling pressure.

## Wind chill

Wind makes cold air feel colder and causes frostbite faster. At -10 °C a 30
km/h wind feels like -20 °C; at -20 °C with 30 km/h it feels like -33 °C, and
exposed skin can freeze in 10 to 30 minutes. Below a wind chill of about -40,
skin can freeze in under 10 minutes.

## Humidex and heat index

Humidity makes heat feel hotter because sweat evaporates less. At 30 °C and
70 percent humidity it feels like about 40; at 35 °C and 50 percent about 45.
A humidex of 40 is uncomfortable, 45 and above is dangerous: rest in shade,
drink water, and watch for heat exhaustion.

## Moon phases

The moon goes from new (not visible) through waxing crescent, first quarter
(right half lit in the northern hemisphere), waxing gibbous, full, waning
gibbous, last quarter (left half lit) and waning crescent, in about 29.5
days. A full moon rises around sunset and sets around sunrise; a new moon
rises and sets with the sun. Full and new moons bring the largest tides.

## Sunrise, sunset and day length

Days are longest at the June solstice (around 21 June) and shortest at the
December solstice (around 21 December) in the northern hemisphere, and the
reverse in the south. At the equinoxes (around 20 March and 22 September)
day and night are about 12 hours everywhere, and the sun rises due east and
sets due west. Twilight after sunset lasts about 30 minutes at mid
latitudes, longer further north.

## Fog and frost

Fog forms on clear, calm nights when the ground cools the air to its dew
point, mostly in valleys and over water, and usually burns off by mid
morning. Frost is likely when the evening is clear and calm and the
temperature is below about 5 °C at sunset: cover tender plants.
