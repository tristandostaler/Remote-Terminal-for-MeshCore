# Useful knots

## How to tie a bowline (fixed loop that will not slip)

For a loop at the end of a rope that does not tighten under load: rescue,
tying around a post or tree, or hanging a bag. Make a small overhand loop
(the "rabbit hole") in the standing part, with the working end on top. Bring
the end up through the hole, around behind the standing part, and back down
through the hole. Pull the standing part to tighten. Easy to untie after
loading. Leave a long tail.

## How to tie a clove hitch (rope to a post)

Quick hitch around a pole, post or rail, easy to adjust. Wrap the rope around
the post, cross over itself, wrap around again, and tuck the end under the
second wrap so both ends come out from the middle in opposite directions.
It can slip if the post turns or the load moves around: add a half hitch for
security.

## How to tie a taut-line hitch (adjustable tent line)

For tent guy lines and tarp lines you can tighten by sliding the knot. Pass
the rope around the stake, bring the end back alongside the standing part,
wrap it twice around the standing part inside the loop, then once more
outside the loop and tuck it through. Slide the knot up to tighten; it holds
under load.

## How to tie a trucker's hitch (tighten a load)

For pulling a rope very tight, such as tying down a load or a ridge line.
Tie a fixed loop in the middle of the rope (a slipped overhand or a
figure-eight loop), pass the end around the anchor hook or tree, bring it
back through the loop, and pull down: this gives a 2 to 1 pulling advantage.
Secure with two half hitches below the loop.

## How to tie two half hitches (tie off a rope end)

General-purpose tie-off to a ring, post or tree. Pass the end around the
object, then around the standing part and through itself (one half hitch),
then repeat in the same direction (two half hitches). Slide them snug. Also
used to finish other knots.

## How to tie a square knot (reef knot) and its limits

Joins two ends of the same rope, for bandages and bundles. Right over left
and under, then left over right and under. Both ends come out on the same
side when it is tied right. It is not safe for joining two ropes that will
carry a load: it can slip or capsize. Use a sheet bend for that.

## How to tie a sheet bend (join two ropes)

Joins two ropes, even of different thickness. Make a bight (U-shape) in the
thicker rope. Pass the thinner rope up through the bight, around behind both
legs of it, and tuck it under itself (not under the bight). Pull tight. A
double sheet bend wraps around twice for extra security.

## How to tie a figure-eight knot (stopper) and figure-eight loop

Figure-eight stopper: stops a rope end slipping through a hole or pulley.
Make a loop, pass the end around behind the standing part, then down through
the loop: it looks like an 8. Figure-eight loop: tie the same knot with the
rope doubled to get a strong fixed loop, used by climbers to tie in. It is
harder to untie after heavy loading than a bowline.

## How to tie a prusik knot (grip a rope)

A loop of thinner cord wrapped around a thicker rope that grips when loaded
and slides when not: used to climb a rope, as a backup, or to hang a tarp
from a ridge line. Wrap the loop around the rope and through itself three
times, keeping the wraps neat and side by side.
