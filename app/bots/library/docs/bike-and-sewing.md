# Bicycle repair and sewing

## Fixing a flat bicycle tire (puncture)

Shift to the smallest rear cog, open the rim brake if it has one, and remove
the wheel (quick-release lever or axle nuts). Let out any air, then push two
tire levers under the tire bead and run one around the rim to free one side.
Pull out the tube, pump a little air into it and listen or feel for the hole
(or hold it under water and look for bubbles). Run your fingers carefully
inside the tire to find and remove the thorn or glass, or the new tube will
puncture too.

## Patching an inner tube

Roughen the area around the hole with the sandpaper from the patch kit, spread
a thin layer of rubber cement a little wider than the patch, and wait until
it is dry and no longer shiny (a few minutes). Press the patch on firmly for a
minute. Self-adhesive patches skip the glue but are less durable. Put a
little air in the tube, fit it inside the tire starting at the valve, push the
tire back onto the rim with your thumbs (avoid levers, they pinch the tube),
and pump it to the pressure printed on the tire's side.

## Bicycle tire pressure

The range is printed on the tire's sidewall. Typical: road bikes 80 to 110 psi
(5.5 to 7.5 bar), hybrid and commuter 50 to 70 psi (3.5 to 5 bar), mountain
bikes 25 to 35 psi (1.7 to 2.4 bar). Heavier riders and smooth roads go
higher; rough trails and wet roads lower. Presta valves (thin) need the small
nut on top unscrewed before pumping; Schrader valves are the same as car
valves.

## Chain fell off, and chain care

Shift to the smallest front chainring, lay the chain onto the lower teeth of
the chainring, lift the rear wheel or hold the derailleur forward, and turn
the pedals backward slowly; it climbs back on. Care: wipe the chain with a rag,
put one drop of chain oil on each roller while turning the pedals backward,
then wipe off the excess, every few hundred kilometres or after rain. A chain
that squeaks needs oil; one that skips may be worn out.

## Adjusting bicycle brakes and gears

Rim brakes: pads should hit the rim squarely, about 1 to 2 mm from it, and
the lever should not touch the handlebar when squeezed. Turn the barrel
adjuster where the cable meets the brake (or the lever) outward to tighten.
Replace pads worn to the grooves. Gears that skip or will not shift up: turn
the rear derailleur's barrel adjuster a quarter turn at a time, outward if it
hesitates moving to bigger cogs, inward if to smaller ones.

## Sewing on a button

Thread a needle with a doubled thread about 50 cm long and knot the ends.
Start from the back of the fabric, come up through one hole and down through
the opposite one, 4 to 6 times per pair of holes. Leave a little slack under
the button (put a pin across it while sewing), then wind the thread around
the stitches under the button a few times to make a shank, push the needle
to the back, and tie off with several small stitches.

## Basic hand stitches

Running stitch: in and out in a straight line, for quick seams and gathering.
Backstitch: strong as machine sewing; each stitch goes back to meet the end
of the previous one. Whip stitch: over the edge, to join two edges or stop
fraying. Ladder (invisible) stitch: small stitches alternating inside each
folded edge, pulled closed to hide a seam, for mending a split seam on the
right side. End every seam with a few tiny stitches in the same place, or a
knot.

## Mending a tear, a hole or a hem

Small tear: on the inside, stitch the edges together with small whip stitches,
or put an iron-on patch behind it. Larger hole: cut a patch bigger than the
hole, fold its edges under, and stitch it on all round. Darning a sock: weave
thread up and down across the hole, then across again, over and under,
like a tiny basket. Fallen hem: fold it back up, pin it, and sew with small
stitches catching only a thread or two of the outer fabric so they do not
show.

## Fixing a zipper

A zipper that separates behind the slider usually has a worn slider: squeeze
the slider's two plates gently together with pliers, a little on each side,
and try it. A stuck zipper moves more easily rubbed with pencil graphite, soap
or candle wax. A missing pull can be replaced with a key ring or a paper clip.
